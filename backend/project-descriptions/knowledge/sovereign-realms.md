## Overview and context
<!-- meta: {"category":"overview","technologies":["Unreal Engine 5.8","C++","Blender","Python"]} -->
Sovereign Realms is a fantasy real-time strategy game set in a voxel diorama world, built by Kyle-Anthony in Unreal Engine 5.8 with C++. The player raises a kingdom (castle, farms, gold mines, barracks, archery ranges, watch towers, a dock and a troop ship), trains swordsmen and archers, and fights an AI commander until one side's castle falls. The same battle can be played two ways: from above as a classic RTS, or on foot in a third-person hero view as King David or Jonathan, the archer prince, leading the troops around you.

It is a personal game project in active development, not a released or distributed game. It has no players, sales or store listing yet, and nothing about it should be described as launched. It runs on macOS and Windows from one codebase, and Kyle develops it across a Mac and a Windows PC with a shared dev branch.

The portfolio shows a 32-second silent showreel recorded from the real game with the HUD hidden, plus in-engine 4K screenshots: wide aerials showing the scale of the island maps, battles, the troop ship and gameplay shots with the interface on screen.

## Gameplay and systems
<!-- meta: {"category":"features","technologies":["Unreal Engine 5.8","C++","Game AI"]} -->
Sovereign Realms plays as a full RTS match. Each side starts with a castle, a gold mine, a watch tower, a small army and one Builder, and grows an economy of gold and food. Builders place and raise buildings and clear rocks, buildings level up, and the castle's level unlocks more, including the second hero. Troops form up to six platoons with leaders and formations (line, column, wedge, square, loose), and take orders to move, attack, patrol, guard or board the troop ship to cross the sea.

The enemy is an AI commander with Easy, Normal and Hard difficulties. It builds its own base, trains an army, and times attack waves; on Hard it also sends flanking parties. Combat covers melee, archer volleys with real arrow flight, tower and castle-corner defenses, razing buildings, and heroes who fall and rise again at the castle. Fog of war hides what your units cannot see, and matches can be saved and loaded.

The hero view puts the player in the battle: King David fights with a sword combo and his guard follows him, Jonathan draws and looses a bow with a crosshair, and a command mode lets the player order the army without leaving the hero's eyes. A gold silhouette shows the player's own units through buildings and terrain.

## Maps and world building
<!-- meta: {"category":"world","technologies":["Unreal Engine 5.8","Blender","Procedural generation","Lumen"]} -->
Sovereign Realms has four battle maps plus a sandbox. Realm v2 is the largest, an island 640 m square with a 184 m mountain, sea arms, rivers, five gold mines, a ruins plateau and a port for each side. Realm v3 is a compact 512 m island designed from lessons of v2: each castle sits on a cliff-ringed plateau with one ramp down to a single mountain pass, a central basin holds two neutral mines, and dense forest fills everything else. The Highlands has a river gorge and a central mesa, and the original Realm is the first battle map. A Test World starts both sides at full strength for balance testing.

Every model and texture in the game, from units and buildings to trees and terrain props, is generated by Python scripts in Blender and imported into Unreal, rather than bought from an asset store. The look relies on Lumen lighting, volumetric fog and stylized water, with graphics presets from High to Low.

## Engineering and performance
<!-- meta: {"category":"architecture","technologies":["C++","Unreal Engine 5.8","Git LFS","Python","Blender"]} -->
Sovereign Realms is written in C++ inside Unreal Engine 5.8, with systems for the battle rules, construction, the enemy commander, fog of war, the troop ship, formations and orders, and the HUD. Units are voxel figures with a custom rig. To keep large battles smooth, each unit's pose is computed once per frame, units out of view are not posed, distant ones update at lower rates, and a spatial hash of all units serves steering, targeting, defenses and arrows. A dedicated optimization pass measured frame times before and after these changes.

The repository uses Git LFS for every Unreal asset, image, mesh and sound, and documents a two-machine workflow (a Mac branch, a Windows branch, a shared dev branch and a production main branch). The game is developed with heavy use of AI coding agents directed by Kyle, who sets the design, reviews every build and makes the calls on gameplay and look.

The showreel and screenshots were produced from the packaged game by scripted camera paths that render frame by frame, so camera motion is smooth regardless of frame rate, then edited to the beat of the music with Python and Blender.
